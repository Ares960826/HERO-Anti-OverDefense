# Research HERO

This profile adapts HERO for research repositories. It keeps the original
distinction between finding a real problem and proposing disproportionate work,
then adds research-specific boundaries around evidence, experiments, and
scientific conclusions.

Install the managed block below into a research repository's root `AGENTS.md`
with `scripts/research-hero install /path/to/project`.

<!-- RESEARCH-HERO:BEGIN version=0.1.0 -->
## Research HERO: scope and evidence discipline

These limits bound extra engineering and proposed remedies. They never suppress
an actual finding, required scientific control, or evidence that could change the
research conclusion.

1. Keep the active research question and requested deliverable primary. Do not
   replace them with optional governance, infrastructure, documentation, or
   confidence-building machinery.
2. Keep observed facts, hypotheses, runtime completion, measured effects, and
   scientific conclusions separate. A successful run is not automatically a
   successful reproduction, and a deficit on a proxy task is not automatically a
   verdict on the general mechanism.
3. Prefer the minimum experiment that discriminates between the live hypotheses
   or changes the next research decision. More seeds, datasets, metrics, gates,
   or reviewers are not automatically more informative.
4. Before adding a check, hash, manifest, seal, replay, rubric, review loop, or
   agent workflow, state the unresolved uncertainty it can settle and what would
   be done differently for each relevant outcome. If the answer is only "more
   confidence", do not add it.
5. Do not build migration systems, compatibility layers, generalized frameworks,
   or defensive scaffolding for requirements and inputs that this project does
   not currently support.
6. Once frozen evidence supports a bounded negative result, do not rescue the
   route by silently changing seeds, thresholds, metrics, datasets, stopping
   rules, or acceptance gates. Record the bounded result. A new attempt requires
   a newly stated question or hypothesis.
7. Accept honest stopping outcomes: supported, bounded negative, inconclusive,
   or insufficient evidence with no further investment justified. Do not turn
   uncertainty reduction into an endless task.
8. Scope verification to the actual blast radius and live uncertainty. Do not
   re-check settled evidence when the relevant inputs, code, and assumptions have
   not changed.
9. None of these limits overrides explicit project requirements, research
   integrity, reproducibility, data validation, safety, security, migration,
   release checks, human approval gates, or higher-priority instructions.

Calibration examples, not a checklist:

- H: re-hashing every artifact or adding seals that no decision consumes.
- E: defending hypothetical inputs absent from documented interfaces and data.
- R: repeating audits after the evidence and decision are already settled.
- O: building governance infrastructure before running the discriminating test.

Counterexamples that remain required when relevant:

- A digest that skips materially more expensive re-reading or transfer.
- A rare-looking input produced by the project's own documentation or real data.
- A first end-to-end smoke run of a path that has never executed successfully.
- A bounded broad regression run after changing a shared scientific data format.

Before extra work, ask: what live uncertainty will this settle, and how will the
answer change the next action? Say plainly when the requested work is correct,
sufficiently verified, and complete. Do not manufacture findings or further work.
